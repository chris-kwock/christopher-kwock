**Scenario:** 21 — Forest Software\
**Market observation date:** November 20, 2026\
**Exposure:** EUR 1,700,000 receivable\
**Settlement horizon:** 182 calendar days\
**Currency convention:** USD per EUR\
**Basis of analysis:** Stage 2 Model Specification and Stage 4 Market-Data and Validation Memo

## **1. Executive summary**

Forest Software expects to receive EUR 1,700,000 from a Finnish customer in 182 days. As a U.S.-based company reporting in U.S. dollars, Forest Software is exposed to the risk that the euro depreciates before settlement, reducing the USD value of its receivable.

Using the supplied market inputs, the forward contract provides the highest locked-in USD proceeds of **$1,989,850**, compared with approximately $1.879 million under the money-market hedge and minimum net proceeds of $1,835,320 under the EUR put option.

However, the assigned forward rate of 1.1705 USD/EUR is materially higher than the covered interest parity (CIP) implied forward rate of approximately 1.1052 USD/EUR. This discrepancy raises concerns regarding the comparability and availability of the assigned forward quotation.

**Recommendation:** Forest Software should pursue a full forward hedge, conditional upon obtaining a current, executable bank quotation. The assigned forward provides the most attractive combination of cash-flow certainty and USD proceeds, but Treasury should not authorize the transaction based solely on the undated scenario quote.

## **3. Hedge calculations**

### **3.1 Unhedged position**

If Forest Software remains unhedged, the company converts the EUR receivable into USD at the prevailing settlement exchange rate.

\\[ \text{Unhedged Proceeds}=FC\_{AMT}\times S_T \\]

\\[ =1,700,000\times S_T \\]

| Settlement scenario   | Exchange rate | USD proceeds  |
| --------------------- | ------------- | ------------- |
| EUR depreciates 5%    | 1.04405       | $1,774,885.00 |
| EUR remains unchanged | 1.09900       | $1,868,300.00 |
| EUR appreciates 5%    | 1.15395       | $1,961,715.00 |

Every 1% change in the exchange rate relative to the initial spot rate changes the USD value of the receivable by $18,683.

The unhedged position requires no upfront hedging expenditure and provides full participation in euro appreciation. However, it exposes Forest Software to potentially significant losses if the euro depreciates.

### **3.2 Forward hedge**

Forest Software enters into a 182-day forward contract to sell EUR 1,700,000 in exchange for USD at the assigned forward rate.

\\[ \text{Forward Proceeds}=FC\_{AMT}\times F_0 \\]

\\[ =1,700,000(1.1705) \\]

## **4. Covered interest rate parity analysis**

Covered interest rate parity provides a theoretical benchmark for evaluating whether the quoted forward exchange rate is consistent with the spot exchange rate and the interest-rate differential between the two currencies.

### **CIP-implied forward rate**

\\[ F\_{implied}=S_0 \frac{1+R\_{USD}T}{1+R\_{FC}T} \\]

\\[ =1.0990 \frac{1+0.0451(182/360)} {1+0.03372(182/360)} \\]

\\[ =\boxed{1.1052168112} \\]

### **Parity difference**

Following the sign convention in the Stage 2 specification:

\\[ Parity\_{Difference}=F\_{implied}-F_0 \\]

\\[ =1.1052168112-1.1705 \\]

The assigned bank forward exceeds the CIP-implied forward by approximately 652.83 pips, or 5.91% relative to the CIP-implied rate.

The parity discrepancy should not automatically be interpreted as an arbitrage opportunity. The Stage 4 memo identifies several limitations:

- The assigned forward quotation is undated and is not confirmed to be executable.
- The spot exchange rate is an ECB reference fixing rather than a tradable bank quotation.
- The USD Treasury yield and EUR Euribor rate represent different types of financial instruments.
- The interest-rate benchmarks have one-year maturities rather than matching the 182-day hedge horizon.
- Actual borrowing rates, lending rates, bid-ask spreads and transaction costs may differ from the model assumptions.

The model correctly returns a REVIEW status under the specified validation rules. The discrepancy reflects inconsistent or unmatched market inputs rather than an identified error in the hedge calculation formulas.

### **Documentation discrepancies**

Three documentation issues were identified.

**Named-range count:** The Stage 2 specification repeatedly requires eleven named input ranges but lists only ten. All ten listed inputs are provided in the Stage 4 memo. No additional input was invented to satisfy the stated count.

**Parity difference sign:** The Stage 2 specification defines the parity difference as the implied forward less the quoted forward. The Stage 4 memo reports the difference in the opposite direction. Both calculations have the same absolute magnitude, but the signs differ. This analysis follows the Stage 2 sign convention.

**Placeholder validation figures:** The original Stage 2 check figures were calculated using different spot rates, interest rates and option strikes. They should not be treated as universal validation targets after the introduction of Stage 4 market inputs.

None of these documentation issues prevents the calculation of the hedge outcomes, but they should be addressed before final model acceptance.

## **9. Executive recommendation**

**To:** Chief Financial Officer, Forest Software\
**From:** Treasury Analysis\
**Subject:** Recommended Foreign Exchange Hedging Strategy

### **Recommendation**

Forest Software should pursue a forward contract covering the entire EUR 1,700,000 receivable, subject to confirmation of an executable forward quotation. Based on the assigned forward rate of 1.1705 USD/EUR, the company would secure $1,989,850 in proceeds at settlement, eliminating uncertainty surrounding the USD value of the receivable.

### **Financial rationale**

The forward hedge provides the highest proceeds across all eleven exchange-rate scenarios evaluated. Compared with the money-market hedge, the forward generates an additional $110,981.42 without requiring the company to borrow euros and invest the converted USD. The forward also guarantees $154,530 more than the minimum net proceeds provided by the put option while avoiding the $32,980 upfront premium.

Although remaining unhedged or purchasing a put option would allow Forest Software to benefit from euro appreciation, the euro would need to appreciate by approximately 6.51% and 8.27%, respectively, before these strategies outperform the assigned forward. Given the company's exposure to euro depreciation, securing predictable operating cash flow is preferable to relying on favorable exchange-rate movements, assuming cash-flow certainty is Treasury's primary objective.

### **Market-data concerns**

The primary concern with the forward hedge is the significant discrepancy between the assigned bank forward and the CIP-implied forward rate. Based on the supplied interest-rate benchmarks, the theoretical forward rate is 1.1052168112 USD/EUR, substantially below the assigned rate of 1.1705 USD/EUR.

Because the assigned forward quotation is undated and the interest-rate benchmarks do not represent matched executable financing rates, Treasury should obtain updated quotations before committing to a transaction. The apparent advantage of the forward hedge may be substantially reduced once current market conditions are reflected.

### **Alternative strategy**

If the assigned forward rate is unavailable, Forest Software should compare the updated bank quotation against a money-market hedge calculated using executable borrowing and lending rates.

Under the supplied benchmark assumptions, the money-market hedge is equivalent to a forward rate of approximately 1.1052168112 USD/EUR. An executable forward above this rate would provide higher nominal proceeds than the benchmark money-market hedge, before considering transaction costs and other financing considerations.

If Treasury prioritizes participation in favorable exchange-rate movements over maximizing guaranteed proceeds, the EUR put option remains a viable alternative. However, its lower guaranteed proceeds and upfront premium make it less attractive under the supplied assumptions.

### **Conclusion**

Forest Software should prioritize eliminating foreign exchange uncertainty rather than attempting to profit from currency movements. A full forward hedge is the preferred strategy based on the supplied inputs, provided that the forward quotation can be confirmed. Treasury should complete the market-data reconciliation before executing the hedge.
